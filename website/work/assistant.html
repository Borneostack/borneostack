Ask about leave, claims, delivery, prices or IT rules

Pick a question or type your own, in English or Malay.

Your question is sent to our server and to an AI service to be answered. It is not stored. To limit use we keep only a count of questions per visitor, under a code that changes every day, never your IP address.

The problem

Staff ask the same questions again and again: how many days of leave, how to claim mileage, what the delivery charge is. The answer is in a PDF that nobody opens, so someone in HR or sales answers by hand. A chatbot that invents an answer is worse than no chatbot, because a wrong leave rule or price gets acted on.

What we built

An assistant that reads a fixed set of documents and nothing else. Each answer shows the passage it came from, with the supporting words highlighted, and a link that opens the PDF at that page. If the documents do not contain the answer, it declines instead of guessing. Try the suggested question about study leave to see it decline.

How it works

This page asks a small server we run. These are the real steps, in the order they happen.

  1. 1. Read the PDFs

    A script reads each PDF page by page and cuts it into 68 short passages. Every passage remembers its document, its page and its heading. A table row keeps its column names, so it still makes sense on its own.

  2. 2. Make them searchable by meaning

    Each passage is turned into a set of numbers that stands for what it means, in English or Malay. That is how a question in Malay can find an English passage.

  3. 3. Find the closest passages

    The question gets the same treatment, and is also searched by its words. The two searches are combined and the best four passages are kept.

  4. 4. Stop if nothing is close

    If even the best passage is not close enough, the answer is "not in the documents". No answer is written, so nothing can be made up.

  5. 5. Write from those passages only

    Otherwise an AI writer is given just those passages. It must reply with short statements, each naming one passage and quoting its words, in the language of the question.

  6. 6. Check before showing

    Our own code then checks every statement. The quote must be in the passage, every figure must be in the quote, and the statement must use the passage’s own words. Anything that fails is dropped. If nothing is left, you see "not in the documents".

How it was checked

On 27 November 2026 we tested the search alone with 29 questions (20 English, 9 Malay) whose answers we knew. The right page was among the four passages handed to the writer for 29 of 29, and was the first for 26. We also asked 6 questions the documents do not cover, such as tomorrow’s weather: the search stopped all 6 before any answer was written. We chose the “close enough” cut-off using these same questions, so this shows that the system works, not how it does on questions it has never seen.

Then 11 of those questions (8 with an answer, 3 without) went through the whole system and we read every answer against the PDFs. All 8 answers were correct and named the right page, and all 3 questions without an answer were declined. We repeated the run after making the final check stricter (it now makes sure each statement uses the cited passage’s own words); all 8 answers still passed. We also tried 2 questions that tell the assistant to print a false rule with a real citation, one in English and one in Malay. It did not print the false rule either time; the check itself was tested on that case with unit tests. Of the 3 questions that sound close to the documents but are not answered in them, 2 reached the writer and it declined both.

It is a small test: more questions would find more mistakes, the check can still miss a statement that reuses the passage’s words to say something different, and the Malay check only knows the vocabulary of these five documents. Malay answers are good on the questions we read, not perfect.

For technical readers: the stack and the limits

Stack. A static Next.js page on Vercel calls one Cloudflare Worker (TypeScript) with a D1 database and Workers AI. Everything runs on the free plan. The PDFs are read by a build script; the Worker never parses a PDF.

Search. Embeddings: @cf/baai/bge-m3, 1024 numbers per passage, stored in D1 as int8 and compared with cosine similarity inside the Worker (68 passages, well under a millisecond). A keyword search (SQLite FTS5) is merged with it by reciprocal rank fusion and the best 4 passages are kept. If the best cosine is below 0.55, the answer is declined without calling the writer.

Writer. @cf/aisingapore/gemma-sea-lion-v4-27b-it (SEA-LION v4, built for Southeast Asian languages), asked for JSON claims with a source number and a quote. Two cheaper models were tried and rejected: Llama 3.1 8B wrote fragments and attached figures to the wrong passage, and Gemma 4 26B used its token limit thinking and returned nothing.

Check. In code: the source must be one that was supplied; the quote must occur in it; each figure must occur in the quote (a figure the visitor typed is also accepted when the quote gives a range it falls in, such as “5 years or more”); more than 60% of the claim’s content words must occur in the passage (Malay words are first mapped to English through a small glossary); a claim that says “not” needs a passage that does too; a claim is at most 300 characters. Citation numbers are added by the code, not by the model.

Cost and limits. One answered question uses about 27 Workers AI neurons on this setup (measured over 8 answers on 27 November 2026). The free allowance is 10,000 a day for the whole account and is shared with the other examples, so the live assistant switches to recorded answers when its share is used up. Each visitor can ask 8 questions in 10 minutes and 30 a day. The Worker does about 70 x 1,024 multiply-adds per question, which fits the free plan’s 10 ms of CPU per request; waiting for the AI does not count.

Not covered. The check compares words, not meaning, so it cannot prove a statement follows from its quote. Scanned PDFs need OCR, and tables spread over several pages need extra work. We would build both in a client project.
